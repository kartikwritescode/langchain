 **Definition and Core Technology:** Generative AI represents a shift from traditional analytical AI by creating entirely new content (such as text, images, code, and video). It is powered by three key architectural breakthroughs: Transformer Models (LLMs), Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), and Diffusion Models.
* **Applications and Economic Impact:** The technology is transforming various industries through applications like automated software engineering, creative media production, and accelerated drug discovery. It is projected to add $2.6 trillion to $4.4 trillion annually to the global economy by driving productivity, democratizing skills, and accelerating R&D.
* **Critical Risks and Challenges:** Implementation of Generative AI introduces major risks that organizations must manage, including model "hallucinations" (confident but incorrect outputs), data privacy leaks, intellectual property and copyright disputes, algorithmic bias, and high environmental impacts (carbon footprints and water usage).
* **Emerging Regulations:** Governments are establishing guardrails to control these risks, highlighted by the EU AI Act (the first comprehensive law categorizing AI by risk levels), the US Executive Order on AI focusing on safety and privacy, and copyright offices ruling that purely AI-generated works cannot be copyrighted without substantial human input. 
* **Future Outlook (2025 and Beyond):** The landscape is transitioning toward multimodal AI (processing multiple data types simultaneously), autonomous AI agents that can execute complex workflows, energy-efficient Small Language Models (SLMs) running locally on devices, and proprietary custom models trained on secure enterprise data.
